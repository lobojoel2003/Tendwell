We're so sorry. Whatever brought you here, whether the death was expected or sudden, you are
probably carrying a great deal right now. This guide is here to make the next few days a little
lighter.

The most important thing to know is this: **very little has to happen today.** Much of what you may
be worrying about can wait for days, weeks or even months. Let's start with what can't.

## The few things that can't wait

**Having the death confirmed.** If your loved one died in a hospital, hospice or care home, the
staff will take care of this. If they died at home and were under hospice care, call the hospice. If
the death at home was sudden or unexpected, call 911.

**Contacting a funeral home.** Once the death has been confirmed, a funeral home can bring your loved
one into their care. You don't need to have decided on a funeral, burial or cremation before you
call. The first conversation is usually short.

**Looking after anyone who depended on them.** If they cared for children, another adult or pets,
make sure those loved ones are safe and with someone familiar.

**Keeping their home safe.** If their home will be empty, lock it, take care of anything that will
spoil, and keep valuables and papers somewhere secure.

That's it for the essentials. Everything else can be done step by step.

## What can wait

It may feel as if you should be calling the bank, cancelling the phone contract and sorting out the
house. You don't need to. In most cases:

- **Banks and creditors** can be told in the coming weeks.
- **Accounts and subscriptions** can be closed over the next few months.
- **The will and the estate** are dealt with over months, sometimes longer.
- **Their belongings** can wait as long as you need, unless a home has to be cleared.

If something truly has a deadline, Tendwell will tell you, calmly and with the actual time limit.
There are only a handful of them.

## Who to tell first

Start with the people closest to them. You don't have to make every call yourself. Ask one or two
people to help share the news with wider family, friends and colleagues.

If they were working, let their employer know when you can. Their manager or HR team can tell
colleagues and explain any pay or benefits the family may receive.

## Getting help from others

People often want to help but don't know how. It's okay to be specific:

- "Could you make a few phone calls for me?"
- "Could you bring dinner on Thursday?"
- "Could you collect the kids from school this week?"
- "Could you look through the mail and make a list of anything that looks important?"

If brothers, sisters or other family members want to share the practical work, a Tendwell plan lets
you divide the steps so everyone can see who is doing what.

## Keeping track

Grief makes it harder to concentrate and remember. That's normal. A few things help:

- Keep a notebook, or use the notes in your plan, to write down who you spoke to and what they said.
- Keep important papers together in one folder.
- Ask organizations to send things in writing.

## When you're ready

When the first few days have passed, the next steps are about letting organizations know, finding
the will and gathering papers. Tendwell will show you just a few things at a time, so the list
never feels bigger than it needs to.

## A note on taking care of yourself

Eat something. Drink some water. Rest when you can. Say yes to offers of help. If it ever feels like
too much, you can call or text 988 in the US to talk to someone, any time of day or night.

You don't have to do this perfectly. You just have to do the next small thing, and then rest.
