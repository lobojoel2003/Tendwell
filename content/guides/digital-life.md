Much of a life now lives online: photos, messages, email, social media and accounts with dozens of
companies. Deciding what to do with them can bring up strong feelings. There's no right answer, and
no need to rush.

## Save first, decide later

Before you close anything, think about what the family might want to keep:

- photos and videos
- messages and voicemails
- documents, writing or creative work
- contact lists, which can help when sharing news
- emails that may be needed for the estate

Closing an account often deletes its contents for good. If you're not sure, leave the account as it
is for now.

## Their phone and computer

Their phone and computer may hold the keys to many other accounts. Before resetting or recycling a
device:

- check whether anyone knows the passcode
- look for a password manager or a written list of passwords
- save photos and files you want to keep
- keep their phone number active for a while if accounts send login codes to it

If no one knows the passcode, it may not be possible to unlock a device. Some manufacturers have a
process for families, which usually requires a death certificate and sometimes a court order.

## Did they leave wishes?

Some services let people decide in advance what happens to their account:

- **Google** has an Inactive Account Manager, which can share data with chosen people or delete the
  account after a period of inactivity.
- **Facebook** lets people choose a legacy contact, who can manage a memorialized profile.
- **Apple** lets people add a Legacy Contact, who can request access to their data after death.

Their will or a note may also include wishes about their online life.

## Memorialize, close or keep

For most services, there are three choices:

- **Memorialize.** Some social networks, such as Facebook and Instagram, can turn a profile into a
  memorial. Friends can still see it and leave messages, but no one can log in.
- **Close.** Most services have a form for family members or the executor to request that an account
  be closed. You'll usually need a death certificate and proof of your relationship.
- **Keep for now.** You can leave an account as it is, especially if you haven't yet saved what
  matters.

Families often choose different options for different accounts. A memorial profile can be a place to
share memories, while an old email account is closed.

## Finding the right process

Most large services have a help page for families. Searching the company's help center for
"deceased", "memorialize" or "bereavement" usually finds it.

Each process is different, and some take weeks. Keep a note of what you requested and when.

## A word about passwords

Even if you know their password, many services ask family members not to sign in as the person, and
their terms of service may not allow it. In most states, a law sets out what an executor can access,
and the person's own wishes can make a difference.

The safest route is usually each service's official process. If an account holds something
important for the estate, a lawyer can advise.

## Subscriptions and online shopping

Online subscriptions can keep charging for years. Look for:

- streaming services for music, film and TV
- app store subscriptions on their phone
- cloud storage
- software and news subscriptions
- online shopping memberships
- regular deliveries and meal kits

Many can be cancelled online or with a short letter. If something was paid in advance, ask for a
refund.

## Email: usually the last to go

Their email inbox is often the best way to find other accounts, because receipts, statements and
password resets all land there. Many families keep it open until most other accounts are closed.

## Looking after your own digital life

Going through someone else's accounts makes many people think about their own. When you're ready,
you might choose a legacy contact, write down where important accounts are, or use a password
manager with an emergency access feature. It's a kind gift to the people who love you.

It's okay if you're not ready to look at their messages or photos yet. They can wait until you are.
