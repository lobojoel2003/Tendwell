After a death, money can come from places the family didn't know about. Some of it needs to be
claimed, and a few claims have time limits. This guide walks through the most common ones.

## Social Security

**Reporting the death.** The funeral home usually tells the Social Security Administration (SSA)
about the death. It's worth checking with them.

**Payments that need to be returned.** If they were receiving Social Security, the payment for the
month of death, and any later months, usually has to be returned. Because payments are made a month
behind, this is often the payment that arrives in the month after the death. If it was paid into a
bank account, the bank will usually send it back. If it came as a check, don't cash it.

**Survivors benefits.** Family members may be able to receive monthly payments based on the
person's work record. Often this includes:

- a widow or widower, usually from age 60, or earlier if disabled or caring for the person's young
  children
- children under 18, or a little older if still in high school
- sometimes a divorced spouse, or a parent who depended on them

**The one-time payment.** A one-time payment of $255 can go to a surviving spouse who lived with
them, or to an eligible child. It generally has to be applied for within two years of the death.

To apply, call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 (TTY 1-800-325-0778) or visit a local office.

## Life insurance

Life insurance is usually paid directly to the beneficiary named on the policy, without waiting for
probate. That often makes it the first money the family receives.

Policies can be easy to miss. Look for:

- policy papers at home
- regular premium payments on bank statements
- group life insurance through their work, a union or a professional association
- small policies attached to credit cards, mortgages or car loans
- policies from past employers

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) runs a free Life Insurance Policy
Locator. You submit a request, and participating insurers search their records.

To claim, the beneficiary contacts the insurer, fills in a claim form and sends a certified copy of
the death certificate.

## Workplace benefits

If they were working, or had retired from an employer, ask the HR team about:

- final pay, unused vacation pay, commissions or bonuses
- group life insurance and accidental death cover
- retirement accounts, like a 401(k) or 403(b)
- pension benefits for a surviving spouse
- health insurance for family members, including COBRA

## Retirement accounts and pensions

Retirement accounts usually go to the beneficiary named on the account, not through the will. Each
provider has its own claim process.

If they received a pension, the payments usually stop, but a surviving spouse may be entitled to a
**survivor pension**. Ask the pension provider.

There are tax rules for inherited retirement accounts that can affect how and when money is taken
out. It's worth talking to a tax professional before you make big decisions.

## Health insurance for the family

If family members were covered through the person's health plan, coverage may end. There are usually
options:

- **COBRA** lets family members keep the same workplace coverage for a while, often up to 36 months
  after a death. You usually pay the full cost. You usually have at least 60 days to choose it.
- **The Health Insurance Marketplace** (HealthCare.gov, or your state's site) usually opens a special
  enrollment period when someone loses coverage.
- **Medicaid and CHIP** may cover children or adults with lower incomes.
- **A surviving spouse's own employer** may let them add family members.

## Veterans benefits

If they served in the military, the VA may provide:

- burial in a national cemetery
- a headstone, marker or medallion
- a burial flag and military funeral honors
- a Presidential Memorial Certificate
- help with burial costs, in some cases
- monthly payments, education or health care for a surviving spouse or children, in some cases

Their discharge papers (often called a DD 214) will help. Veterans service officers can help with
the forms for free.

## Other places to look

- **Unclaimed property.** States hold forgotten bank accounts, refunds and insurance payouts. You can
  search for free at MissingMoney.com and on each state's official unclaimed property website.
- **Prepaid funeral plans.** If they bought one, it may cover some or all of the funeral.
- **Credit card benefits.** Some cards include travel or accident insurance.
- **Refunds.** Prepaid subscriptions, memberships, insurance premiums and deposits can often be
  refunded.

## Keeping a record

For each claim, note who you spoke to, when, what they asked for and what happens next. The notes in
your Tendwell plan are a good place for this, and others in the family can see them too.

It's okay to ask organizations to explain things again, or to put them in writing. You're not
expected to know how any of this works.
