When the busy early weeks are over, many people find that grief arrives more fully. That's normal,
and it's a good time to think about what support might help.

1. **Hospice bereavement programs.** Hospices usually offer support to families for about a year
   after a death. Many also welcome people from the wider community.
2. **Support groups.** Meeting others who understand can help. There are groups for losing a
   partner, a parent, a child, or someone to suicide or overdose. Libraries, hospitals and faith
   communities often know of local ones.
3. **Talking to a professional.** A grief counselor or therapist can help, especially if things feel
   stuck. Your doctor can point you to someone.
4. **Your workplace.** Many employers offer free, confidential counseling through an employee
   assistance program.
5. **If things feel too heavy,** you can call or text 988 in the US, at any time, to talk to someone.

It's okay to grieve in your own way, and on your own timeline. There's no finish line.
