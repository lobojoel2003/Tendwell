If a spouse or children were on their health plan, it's worth checking where things stand soon, so
no one is left without coverage.

1. **Ask their employer's HR team or the insurer** when the family's coverage will end.
2. **Look out for a COBRA notice.** COBRA lets family members keep the same workplace coverage for a
   while, often up to 36 months after a death. You usually pay the full cost yourselves, which can
   be expensive.
3. **Compare other options.** Losing coverage usually opens a special enrollment period on the
   Health Insurance Marketplace (HealthCare.gov or your state's site), often for 60 days. A plan
   there may cost less, especially with financial help.
4. **Check other options.** A surviving spouse may be able to join their own employer's plan.
   Children may qualify for Medicaid or the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP).
5. **Cancel their own coverage.** Medicare usually ends automatically once Social Security knows
   about the death. Private plans need to be told.

It's okay to ask someone else in the family to compare plans. It's the kind of task that's easier
for someone with a little distance.
