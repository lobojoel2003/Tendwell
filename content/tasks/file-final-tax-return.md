The final return covers their income from January 1 up to the date they died. It's filed in much
the same way as any other year's return.

1. **Find their recent tax returns.** Last year's return shows what kinds of income they had and who
   prepared it.
2. **Collect this year's tax forms.** W-2s, 1099s and similar forms arrive early in the new year,
   usually by mail.
3. **Decide who files.** Usually the executor. A surviving spouse can often file a joint return for
   the year of death.
4. **File the federal and any state returns.** Write "deceased", their name and the date of death
   across the top of the federal return, as the IRS instructions explain.
5. **Claiming a refund.** If a refund is due and you are not a surviving spouse filing jointly, the
   IRS may ask for Form 1310 along with the return.
6. **Ask about the estate's own return.** If the estate earns income after the death, such as
   interest or rent, it may need to file a separate return (Form 1041).

It's okay to hand this to a tax professional. Many families do, and the fee can usually be paid
from the estate.
