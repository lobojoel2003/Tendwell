Life insurance is usually paid directly to the beneficiary named on the policy, so it doesn't
normally wait for probate. That can make it one of the first sources of money for the family.

1. **Look for policies.** Check their papers, bank statements for regular premium payments, and
   emails from insurers.
2. **Ask their employers.** Many workplaces, and some unions and professional groups, provide group
   life insurance. Past employers may too.
3. **Check other places.** Credit cards, mortgages and car loans sometimes come with small life
   insurance policies.
4. **Use the free policy locator.** The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC) runs
   a free Life Insurance Policy Locator that asks insurers to search for policies.
5. **Make the claim.** The beneficiary contacts the insurer, fills in a claim form and sends a
   certified copy of the death certificate.
6. **Ask about payout options.** You can usually take a lump sum. You don't have to decide on the
   spot.

It's okay to take your time before deciding what to do with the money. Many people find it helps to
keep it somewhere safe and talk to a financial adviser first.
