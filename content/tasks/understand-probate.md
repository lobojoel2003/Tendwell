The _estate_ is everything they owned and owed. _Probate_ is the court process that confirms who can
handle it. It sounds daunting, but for many families it is mostly paperwork.

1. **Check the will for an executor.** If there is no will, a court usually appoints an
   _administrator_, often the spouse or a grown child.
2. **Work out what goes through probate.** Many things pass outside it, including:
   - jointly owned property and joint bank accounts
   - life insurance and retirement accounts with a named beneficiary
   - accounts marked payable-on-death or transfer-on-death
   - anything held in a living trust
3. **Ask about a simpler process.** Many states have a quicker process, sometimes called a _small
   estate affidavit_ or _summary administration_, when the estate is below a certain value. The
   limit varies a lot by state.
4. **Contact the probate court** in the county where they lived. Court staff can't give legal advice,
   but they can explain the forms and fees.
5. **Get the court's paperwork.** Once appointed, the executor receives a document, often called
   _letters testamentary_ or _letters of administration_. Banks and others will ask to see it.

It's okay to ask a probate lawyer for help. Many offer a free first conversation, and some
estates are simple enough to handle without one.
