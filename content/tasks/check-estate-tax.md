This step is mostly about ruling things out. For the great majority of families, the answer will be
"no tax is due".

1. **Federal estate tax.** This only applies to estates worth many millions of dollars. The limit is
   set by Congress and changes over time. An accountant can confirm the current figure.
2. **State taxes.** A small number of states charge their own estate tax, and a few charge an
   inheritance tax on what people receive. Their limits can be much lower than the federal one, so
   check the state where they lived and any state where they owned property.
3. **A note for surviving spouses.** Even when no tax is owed, a surviving spouse may benefit from
   filing a federal estate tax return to keep their partner's unused exemption for later. This is
   called _portability_. Ask an accountant whether it makes sense for you.
4. **Ask for help if the estate is large or complicated,** for example if it includes a business,
   farmland or property in several states.

It's okay to feel unsure about this. A short conversation with an accountant or estate lawyer is
usually enough to know whether it applies.
