Once an executor or administrator is appointed, a dedicated account is the cleanest way to handle the
estate's money: bills go out of it, and refunds, final paychecks and sale proceeds go into it.

1. **Get a tax ID number for the estate.** The executor can usually apply for a free Employer
   Identification Number (EIN) for the estate on the IRS website. It takes a few minutes. Banks ask
   for it instead of the person's Social Security number.
2. **Gather what the bank will ask for.** Usually a certified death certificate, the court's letters
   testamentary or letters of administration, the EIN and the executor's ID.
3. **Open the account in the estate's name.** It's often called something like "Estate of Jane Doe,
   John Doe, Executor".
4. **Move the estate's money into it** as accounts are closed.
5. **Pay estate bills from it,** never from your own account if you can help it, and keep every
   receipt. Clear records protect the executor if anyone has questions later.

It's okay to keep it simple. A basic checking account is usually all the estate needs.
