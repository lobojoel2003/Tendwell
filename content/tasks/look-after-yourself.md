  Grief is exhausting. Eating, resting and letting others help are part of the plan, not a
  distraction from it.

In the first days, there can be a lot to do and very little time to stop. It's okay to put some of
it down.

1. **Eat something and drink water.** Small, simple meals count. If someone offers to bring food,
   say yes.
2. **Rest when you can.** Sleep may be broken for a while. Lying down with your eyes closed still
   helps.
3. **Say yes to specific offers of help.** "Could you make some phone calls?" or "Could you pick up
   the kids?" is easier for people than "Let me know if you need anything."
4. **Write things down.** Grief makes it harder to remember. A notebook, or the notes in this plan,
   can hold details so you don't have to.
5. **Don't make big decisions you don't need to make.** Selling a home or giving away belongings can
   wait.
6. **Reach out if it feels too heavy.** In the US, you can call or text 988 at any time to talk to
   someone at the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

It's okay to cry, and it's okay not to. It's okay to laugh at a memory. There is no right way to
feel right now.
