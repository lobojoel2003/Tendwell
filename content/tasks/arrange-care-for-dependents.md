When someone dies, the people they cared for need comfort and routine as much as anything practical.
For now, the goal is simply that they are safe and with someone familiar.

1. **Arrange care for the next few days.** A relative, close friend or trusted neighbor can step in
   while the family works out a longer plan.
2. **Look for their wishes.** A will often names a guardian for children under 18. Some people leave
   a letter or tell a close friend what they would want.
3. **Tell the people who need to know.** Let schools, childcare, day programs or carers know what has
   happened, so they can offer support and know who to contact.
4. **Think about the longer term when you can.** If the children's other parent is living, they
   usually become the main carer. If not, a court may need to confirm a guardian. A family lawyer can
   explain the options in your state.

If the person they cared for was an adult, such as an elderly parent or a family member with a
disability, contact their doctor, social worker or local adult services. They can help arrange
support.

It's okay for children to see that you are sad. Simple, honest words help. You don't need perfect
answers to their questions.
