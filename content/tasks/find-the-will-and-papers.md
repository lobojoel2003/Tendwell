Finding the will tells you who is meant to handle their affairs, and it can also include wishes
about the funeral and the care of children or pets.

1. **Search at home first.** Desks, filing cabinets, a home safe, and folders marked "important" or
   "legal". Look for a lawyer's business card or letters from a law firm.
2. **Ask their lawyer.** Lawyers often keep the original will in their office. If you find a copy,
   the lawyer who wrote it may hold the original.
3. **Check a safe deposit box.** The bank can explain how family members can look inside for a will.
   Rules vary by state and bank.
4. **Ask the local probate court.** In some places, people can deposit a will with the court for
   safekeeping.
5. **Keep the original safe.** The executor will usually need the original, not a copy, to start
   probate. Don't remove staples or write on it.

While you look, set aside other papers you come across: insurance policies, bank statements,
property deeds, car titles, tax returns and any notes about passwords or accounts.

It's okay if you don't find a will. Many people don't have one. State law then decides who inherits,
and a court will appoint someone to handle the estate.
