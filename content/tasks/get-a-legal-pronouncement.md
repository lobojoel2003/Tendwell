If your loved one died in a hospital, hospice facility or nursing home, the staff will take care of
this for you, and you can move on to the next step.

If they died at home, someone with the authority to do so needs to confirm the death. This is often
called a _pronouncement_. It is what allows the death certificate to be completed later.

1. **If they were receiving hospice care**, call the hospice. A hospice nurse can usually come to the
   home, confirm the death and help you contact a funeral home. There is no need to call 911.
2. **If the death was expected but they were not in hospice**, call their doctor and ask what they
   would like you to do. In many areas you will be asked to call 911 or the non-emergency police line.
3. **If the death was sudden or unexpected**, call 911. Emergency services and sometimes the police
   or the medical examiner will come. This is routine, and it does not mean anyone has done
   anything wrong.

It's okay to take a few moments before you make the call, if it was expected. You can sit with them,
open a window, or call someone to be with you. Nothing needs to be rushed.
