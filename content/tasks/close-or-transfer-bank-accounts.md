By now the bank should already know about the death. This step is about what happens to the money.

1. **Joint accounts.** The surviving account holder usually just shows a death certificate, and the
   bank removes the name. The money stays where it is.
2. **Accounts with a named beneficiary.** The beneficiary usually claims the money directly, with ID
   and a death certificate.
3. **Accounts only in their name.** The executor usually closes these and moves the money into the
   estate account. The bank will ask to see the court's paperwork.
4. **Before closing anything,** check whether money is still coming in, such as a pension or a tax
   refund, or going out, such as a mortgage. Move those to the estate account first.
5. **Ask for a closing statement** for your records.
6. **Close or cancel their cards.** Credit cards and debit cards should be cancelled. The balance on
   a credit card is usually paid by the estate, not by the family.

It's okay if this takes a few visits or calls. Bank processes after a death are often slow, even
when everything is in order.
