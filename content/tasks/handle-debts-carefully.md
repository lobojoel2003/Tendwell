This is one of the most important things to know: **in most cases, family members are not
personally responsible for a loved one's debts.** The estate pays what it can.

1. **You may be responsible if** you co-signed a loan, held a joint account or card with them, or
   live in a community property state (for spouses). A lawyer can tell you what applies.
2. **Make a list of debts** from statements, mail and their credit report: credit cards, loans,
   medical bills, a mortgage or car loan.
3. **Let creditors know about the death** and give them the executor's name.
4. **Don't pay from your own pocket.** Wait until the executor knows what the estate has. If there
   isn't enough, state law sets out the order debts are paid, and some may go unpaid.
5. **Know your rights with collectors.** A debt collector shouldn't tell you that you have to pay
   from your own money if you don't, and you can ask them to contact the executor instead.
6. **Some debts are cancelled.** Federal student loans are usually discharged when the borrower dies,
   once a death certificate is provided.

It's okay to say "Please send that to me in writing" and end a call. You never have to agree to
anything over the phone.
