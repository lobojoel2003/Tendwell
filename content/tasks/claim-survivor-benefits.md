If they worked and paid Social Security taxes, their family may be entitled to benefits based on
their record. It's worth asking, even if you aren't sure you qualify.

1. **Who may be eligible.** Often:
   - a widow or widower, usually from age 60, or earlier if they are disabled or caring for the
     person's young children
   - children under 18, or a little older if still in high school
   - in some cases, a divorced spouse or a parent who depended on them
2. **The one-time payment.** A $255 lump-sum payment can go to a surviving spouse who lived with
   them, or to an eligible child.
3. **How to apply.** Call Social Security at 1-800-772-1213 or visit a local office. Survivors
   benefits usually can't be applied for entirely online.
4. **What to have ready.** The death certificate, their Social Security number, your own Social
   Security number and ID, and your marriage certificate or the children's birth certificates.
5. **If you already get Social Security,** ask whether switching to a survivors benefit would give
   you more.

It's okay to ask Social Security to check what you might be owed. They can look at the options for
you.
